I am not a designer. For the longest time, picking colours - for a slide deck, a side project, or just what to wear in the morning - was pure guesswork. I would stare at a colour wheel, read about complementary and triadic schemes, and still end up with something that looked slightly off.

Then I landed on a rule that hasn't failed me yet:

> If a colour combination exists in nature, it works.

Nobody has ever looked at a tree and thought the brown clashes with the green. We have been staring at these combinations our entire lives. They feel right because they are the original palettes - everything else is a remix.

So instead of inventing colour schemes, I just steal them. Here are a few of my favourites, and what they look like when you wear them.

### 📐 Nature Gets the Proportions Right Too

Look at the bars under each picture. Nature never uses its colours equally. A sunflower field is mostly green, with a band of yellow and a small brown centre. A tree is mostly leaves, some trunk and a little shadow.

Designers have a name for this - the **60-30-10 rule**. One dominant colour, one supporting colour, and a small accent. Nature was following it long before anyone wrote it down.

This is the part people usually get wrong. The colours can be right, but if you use them in equal amounts, it looks like a flag. Put the dominant colour on the biggest piece, and save the accent for something small - shoes, a belt, a watch strap.

### 🧭 How to Use This

1. **Find a scene you love.** A walk outside, a photo from a trip, a fruit on your kitchen counter.
2. **Pick two or three colours** from it. Not more.
3. **Notice how much of each** there is. That is your 60-30-10.
4. **Read it top to bottom.** Nature often hands you the outfit order for free - sky on top, ground below.

Next time you are stuck in front of the wardrobe, don't open a colour wheel. Go for a walk.
